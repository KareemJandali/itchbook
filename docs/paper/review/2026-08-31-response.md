bands". The report is [`2026-08-31-referee-report.md`](2026-08-31-referee-report.md),
committed byte-for-byte as received and not edited. The machine-readable index is

The reviewer's own disclosure is recorded rather than discounted. They helped

Every item below states the deviation, then gives one of **fixed**, **open**

## Major comments

### M1 — the calibration is circular and the paper never says so

**Accepted in full. Half fixed now; the decisive half is open.**

*The deviation.* `calibrate_intensity` builds λ̂'s exposure denominator by running
the A-S maker itself (`tools/calibrate_intensity.cpp:57`), and the maker needs a
k before one has been measured. Every k in §6.3 is therefore one iteration of a
fixed-point map from a guess. No convergence check exists, and §6 said nothing
about the iteration at all. The reviewer is right that this is the only comment
in the report that could move numbers.

*Fixed now, the disclosure, and it turns out sharper than expected.* §6.3 is
generated, so the statement is generated as well, from the artifacts and not
typed: all four calibrations bootstrap from **γ = 0.005, k = 200.0**. The
distance travelled in one step is not uniform, and the pattern is unflattering:

| symbol | median fitted k | bootstrap k | one-step move |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| STOR | 203.5 | 200.0 | 1.02× |
| MSFT | 327.2 | 200.0 | 1.64× |
| AMD | 483.9 | 200.0 | 2.42× |
| GOOG | 12.3 | 200.0 | **16.26×** |

STOR is essentially already at a fixed point. GOOG sits sixteen-fold away from
its own starting point, and GOOG is the single symbol carrying §8.3's wide-floor
case, the one whose measured k puts the half-spread floor eight ticks out. The
symbol the central mechanism rests on is the symbol whose calibration is furthest
from convergence. That is now in print, in §6.3, above the table it qualifies.

*Also fixed: the second conditioning, which no rerun repairs.* §6.3 now states
that integrating depth as the mid moves is correct for exposure and is a
different conditioning from the one A-S consumes. λ(δ) enters the spread formula
as the fill rate for an order *placed* at δ, and much of the deep-δ exposure here
comes from orders the mid walked away from, which are adversely selected moments.
The bias is not shown to be neutral, and the paper no longer implies that it is.

*Open, and it is the item that settles this.* One recalibration pass per symbol
under the measured parameters, reporting how far k moves. Compute and the
licensed feed are the only blockers; the design is not one. The split the
reviewer suggests, exposure from orders placed at a depth against exposure from
orders that drifted there, needs a new field on the exposure accumulator in
`intensity.hpp` and is the better test. It is the second pass and not the first.

*What I will not do.* Grade the result as unaffected before the pass has run. If
k moves a lot on GOOG then §8.3's mechanism is conditioned on a number that has
not converged, and that has to be findable in this document before the pass
rather than after it.

### M3 — max drawdown was promised and never delivered

**Accepted in full. Open, and the artifact schema is the blocker.**

*The deviation.* Plan item 11.3.5 commits to markouts at three horizons, an
inventory path plotted, and max drawdown reported. Each of the three was checked:

- **Markouts.** All three horizons *are* in the artifact, as `markout_100ms`,
  `markout_1s` and `markout_10s`, and only `markout_1s` reaches a table. The gap
  is one of reporting and not of measurement, and it is the cheapest of the three
  to close.
- **Inventory path.** Not plotted, and the reviewer is right that the γ figure is
  a sweep and not a path. `InventoryPath`
  (`include/itchbook/sim/inventory_strategies.hpp:92`) is a time-weighted
  accumulator: it produces mean, σ and max\|q\|, and retains no series. No path
  exists in the artifact to plot, so what this needs is a sampled series emitted
  and not a chart drawn.
- **Max drawdown.** Absent from every table, from the artifact and from the
  artifact's schema; the reviewer's "not even as an artifact key" is exact.
  `KillSwitch::drawdown()` (`include/itchbook/risk/kill_switch.hpp:300`) returns
  peak-minus-*current* and not the running maximum, so even the machinery that
  exists does not compute the pre-registered quantity. It needs a max tracker, a
  field on the run record, and a re-run of all 288 runs.

*Open, all three*, and each needs the experiment re-run because each needs a new
field in `as-experiment.json`. Nothing here can be back-computed from what is
committed.

*Fixed now, in the sense that matters most.* §8.5 carries the deviations
explicitly. The reviewer's alternative was "either add them, or add a short
section listing deviations from the pre-registered design with reasons", and it
is right that the list is fine while the silence is not. This document together
with the §8.5 items is that list. A paper that makes a point of transcription
fidelity in §7.5 cannot shed line items from the same pre-registration two
clauses over without a word, and it did.

### M4 — P4 is graded kept on thin evidence

**Accepted in part. The record is corrected; the verdict does not move; the
ladder is open.**

*Where the reviewer is right.* Two points is not a sweep, and calling it a
latency sweep in prose while grading a verdict on it is the third of the three
silent drifts. 19 of 36 is 52.8% against a bare-majority bar, one cell away from
falsified, and the cells are correlated within a symbol-day, which is what M5 is
about. The edge-based companion (25 of 36 overall, 4 of 12 on STOR) is the more
convincing test and already carries a label saying it was not pre-registered.

*Where the report's diagnosis is wrong, and it matters for the fix.* The plan
pointed at "the existing `latency_sweep`", and that tool structurally cannot
grade P4. `tools/latency_sweep.cpp` is built on `backtest.hpp` and
`strategies.hpp`, the **open-loop** phase-6 path, with arms `touch-maker`,
`patient-maker`, `crosser`, `far-quoter`. It has no A-S arm and no closed loop,
and §4 is the section explaining why an inventory-aware strategy cannot be run
down that path at all. The pre-registration named a tool that could not do the
job. That is a defect in the plan. The honest response to it was to say so in
print and build the ladder; what happened instead is that two `as_experiment`
invocations were run and the prose kept the plan's word "sweep".

*Open.* `as_experiment` takes one `--latency-ns` per invocation, so a proper
ladder is N artifacts under the existing `validation/as-experiment*.json` naming
convention that `paper-report.py` already globs. The grader needs no change to
consume five points instead of two. That is the fix and it is cheap.

*Declined: regrading P4 now.* The bar was pre-registered as a fractional equity
change between latencies, and this repository's standing rule is that the bar
does not move once the data is in. §7.5 already carries the caveat that P4's
verdict rests on equity while most of equity is drift. What changes is the
*language*: a two-point comparison is not called a sweep anywhere it is graded,
and the "kept" verdict is annotated as the bare and correlated majority it is.
That annotation lands with the ladder, in the same commit, because writing "bare
majority" while the fix is one command away would be settling for the cheaper
half.

### M5 — the 36 of 36 language overstates the sample

**Accepted in full. Fixed.**

*The deviation.* The paper never pools and claims no significance, which the
reviewer credits. A count out of 36 nonetheless reads as a sample of 36, and four
lanes of one symbol-day are four scorings of one feed on one stock on one day.
The reviewer's evidence is the strongest available: on GOOG, three of the four
calibration lanes come back **bit-identical** (Q1 below). The honest replication
unit is 9 symbol-days, arguably 3 symbols.

*Fixed.* A count out of 36 appears in generated prose in two places, the
abstract's findings block and §8.1, and both now carry the denominator
explicitly. The denominator is computed rather than typed, so it tracks the
symbol and day counts if either changes. The result survives being counted
honestly. The reviewer's own sentence is the right standard and the paper now
meets it.

## Answers to the three questions

**Q1: is the GOOG lane coincidence queue degeneracy or a copy error?**
Degeneracy, confirmed from the artifacts. The confirmation is stronger at
calibration than the report claims and weaker in the experiment.

At calibration (`validation/intensity-GOOG.json`) the optimistic, `mbo` and
pessimistic lanes are **bit-identical**: A = 0.37730153, k = 12.3013, R² =
0.904053, 7,645 maker fills, 46,504 order-seconds, all three. Only `naive`
differs. On a book averaging 60.7 ticks wide with 50 shares at the touch, a
100-share order is essentially always alone at its price level, so front of
queue, tracked queue position and back of queue are the same position. That is
the mechanism, and the paper should say it in one sentence rather than leave a
reader to suspect a `cp`.

In the *experiment* the coincidence is partial, because the closed loop diverges
once fills differ. Across GOOG's 24 three-arm cells, optimistic and `mbo` agree
exactly in 8, and all three queue-aware lanes agree in 2. On MSFT and STOR they
never agree, in any cell. So "identical to four decimals" is exactly right for
§6.3 and an overstatement for §7.1, where the lanes are close but distinct: GOOG
· 2019-12-30 reads 65,823 / 65,768 / 65,785 µ$ per share. §6.3's sentence is
written; §7.1's is open.

**Q2: does the strategy see its fill before or after the book update from the
event that caused it?** After the book update, and before its own decision.
`ClosedLoopBacktest::on_message` (`include/itchbook/sim/closed_loop.hpp:95`) runs
in this order: `book::apply_ex` applies the message to the book (line 111);
`queue_.commit` and `apply_price_priority` resolve our fills against it; each
fill is delivered to the strategy via `deliver(f)` (line 134); and only then is
the strategy's decision invoked with `view.book` pointing at the already-updated
book. The strategy therefore sees a fill on this message, and the market as of
this message, at the same decision point, and never any state from a later one.

That ordering is deliberate and the code says so at line 130: delivering the fill
after the decision would give the strategy a view one event stale in exactly the
moments that matter most. What it is instead is a zero-reaction-time idealisation
at the headline `latency_ns` of 0, which is the reason the 500,000 ns artifact
exists and the reason M4's ladder matters. It is not the classic closed-loop
lookahead, because lookahead requires seeing a *future* message. The reviewer is
right that §4 has to state it. **Fixed:** §4 now does.

**Q3: what γ and k did the bootstrap run with, and are they recorded?**
γ = 0.005 and k = 200.0, in every calibration, and **yes, they are recorded**.
`calibrate_intensity.cpp:139` writes them into each artifact as `gamma` and
`k_assumed`, and they are present in all four committed
`validation/intensity-*.json`. The provenance rule is not broken: the committed
artifact has no unrecorded input.

What was broken is the disclosure. The values were in the artifacts and in no
sentence of the paper, so a reader who did not open the JSON could not know the
measurement had a bootstrap at all, which is M1 restated from the other end.
Being recorded and being disclosed are different properties and only the first
was true. §6.3 now prints both, generated from the artifacts.

## What this review does and does not close

The Phase 11 done-list has one item that cannot be closed by writing code: §4,
the band-over-worlds methodology, reviewed by someone who did not write it. This
review closes it, and §8.5 states the close with its limits attached rather than
as a tick:

- The reader wrote neither the paper nor the code, and had seen no results.
- The reader is **not blind**: they helped sketch the §11.3 experimental design,
  disclosed it unprompted in the first paragraph, and that is recorded in
  `review.json` rather than left in prose.
- The review raised two items bearing on §4, Q2 on the fill/book ordering and M5
  on lanes within a symbol-day not being independent draws, and **did not
  contest the band-over-worlds construction itself**. A review that engaged with
  the construction and found it sound would be evidence that it is sound. This
  one is evidence that a competent reader with the code in front of them did not
  object to it, which is weaker, and §8.5 says which of the two it is.

Four findings remain open and are carried in §8.5 rather than here, because a
"what remains" list that omits what a referee found is the same failure as a
status banner that outlives its results: the calibration fixed point (M1), the
ETF (M2), max drawdown with the inventory path and the two unreported markout
horizons (M3), and the latency ladder (M4). Each needs the experiment re-run
against the licensed feed. None of them is a disagreement.
